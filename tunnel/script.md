# The $70 Million Tunnel Heist — narration script (v1)

Target: about 3:50 of narration (4-minute video).

---

On a Monday morning in August 2005, staff at Brazil's Central Bank in the city of Fortaleza opened the vault and found a hole in the floor. Around seventy million dollars was gone. No alarm had gone off. No guard had seen a thing. And the thieves already had a two-day head start.

The story starts three months earlier, and a few doors down the street.

A group of men rented a small house and hung up a sign. They said they were starting a landscaping business, selling plants and artificial grass. Neighbours watched them carry out sacks of soil almost every day. For a gardening company, that looked completely normal.

It wasn't garden soil. It was the street.

Inside the house, the men were digging. Their tunnel dropped four metres underground and then ran straight toward the bank, about eighty metres away, underneath one of the busiest avenues in the city. The traffic above covered the noise.

And this was no rough hole in the ground. The tunnel was only about seventy centimetres wide, but the walls were lined with wood and plastic sheeting. It had electric lights. This crew had done its homework.

They also knew exactly what they were digging toward. The vault held used banknotes that had been pulled out of circulation to be checked. Old bills. Mixed serial numbers. Nothing in order, and almost impossible to trace.

On the first weekend of August, with the bank closed, they made the final move. They cut up through more than a metre of steel-reinforced concrete and climbed into the vault. The sensors never triggered.

Then came the hard part. The money weighed more than three tons. Bundle by bundle, they dragged it back through the tunnel to the house. By the time the bank opened on Monday, they were gone.

Brazil had just seen the biggest bank robbery in its history. And for a moment, it looked like the perfect crime.

It wasn't.

The first cracks appeared within days. Police stopped a truck carrying cars across the country, and found millions in cash hidden inside the vehicles. Other members of the gang started spending. Arrests followed, one after another.

But the police were not the only ones hunting them. Word spread fast that these men were sitting on a fortune, and other criminals came looking.

Two months after the robbery, the man believed to be one of the leaders was kidnapped. His family paid the ransom. He was found dead anyway. Over the next months, relatives of other suspects were kidnapped too, and each time the families paid.

In the end, dozens of people were arrested. But most of the money never came back. Only a fraction of it has ever been recovered.

Three months of digging. One quiet weekend. Seventy million dollars. And almost nobody who touched it got to keep it.

## Facts to keep straight
- Date: weekend of 6–7 August 2005, discovered Monday 8 August.
- Amount: about 164.8 million reais, roughly US$70 million.
- Tunnel: about 80 m long, 4 m below the street, about 70 cm wide, wood panels, plastic sheeting, electric lights; about three months of digging.
- Cover: a fake gardening / artificial turf business in a rented house.
- Aftermath: suspected leader kidnapped October 2005, ransom paid, found dead; further ransom kidnappings of suspects' relatives; dozens arrested; only part of the money recovered.
- Still to double-check before recording: the "more than three tons" weight, the vault floor thickness, and the car-transporter seizure.
